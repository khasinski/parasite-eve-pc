# Imported RE2 scene scripts

## Implementation plan

1. **Keep the room VM authoritative.** Decode the original instruction, resolve the actor selected by the current task, and record its pending action against its room slot. Keep authored writes to position, facing, scratch words, camera and foreground flags in the VM. The host consumes the resulting state. It does not decide which story branch runs.
2. **Use one actor command path across rooms.** Start with direct playback and the ordinary destination modes. Make the commands replace each other, suspend and resume, and relinquish control on return. Clear pending work when the room initializes or a new actor replaces the slot. Keep arrival flags tied to reaching the destination, including walks that end at world origin. Suppress field pursuit while a script owns a placement so an actor cannot fight its authored direction.
3. **Resolve clips by actor slot.** Use the actor's stock bank and the room binding for the active scenario. Allow the renderer to draw an actor selected by the script even when its ID is above the normal enemy range. Reuse the same world pose and fixed-camera renderer. Check model compatibility and frame bounds before sampling. Keep the camera, mask groups, and effect paths independent of animation state.
4. **Make scene state inspectable.** Extend the existing enemy movement readout with the selected clip, elapsed frame, destination and pause state. Keep unsupported instructions visible in the existing VM counters. A later parity pass can identify where a scene breaks by looking for the first command that fails to affect state.
5. **Finish specialized commands in groups.** Wire neck targets and attachments once the imported renderer can apply them consistently. Add weapon pose, muzzle and sound handling through the shared effect path. Add explicit loop, hold, completion and root-motion semantics using the original sequence data, then send completion back to the VM for scripts that wait on it. Connect player and partner pose commands through the host's rig adapters.
6. **Verify by opcode across scenes.** Use the existing import store and a separate profile. For each scene, record the procedure, actor slot, command, selected asset, first and last pose, arrival flag, camera, and frame at which player control returns. Compare matched renders for mask coverage and retain captures only for useful regressions.

## Movement and animation parity

1. Audit each disc and scenario for actor movement and motion commands. Group uses by opcode, routine, selected work slot, actor class and bank. Cover playable actors, partners such as Ada and Sherry, scenario NPCs such as Kendo and Marvin, scripted enemies and scene objects. Record which scripts write destination operands after issuing the command and which wait for room flags, pose completion or player input.
2. Use the RE2 actor routine as the contract for each movement mode. Decode the operands, then reproduce its turning, translation, tolerances and completion at script tick rate. Use Kendo's intro and window attack as the first traces. Preserve script writes, camera cuts and control ownership as the routines advance. Keep unrelated modes on their existing behavior until their contract is verified.
3. Resolve the original animation sequence on the same clock. Carry per-frame root displacement through room coordinates, including rotation, reverse playback, looping and holds. Separate the RE2 world transform and completion from pose rendering. Match arrival flags and pose completion to retail before switching further room scripts onto the new routines.
4. Map original clips to Aya's rig by bank and move, using equivalent PE poses where they match and retargeting joints for the remaining actions. Use imported clips directly on compatible RE2 actors. Give each actor slot independent command, pose and completion state. Suspend partner following or enemy AI while an authored routine owns that actor, then restore its normal behavior when the script releases it. Route scene objects through their own routine and animation contracts. Keep the visual mapping independent of position, facing and script timing.
5. Trace comparable retail and imported frames through Kendo, the Marvin scene, the Licker reveal and boss entrances. Compare room, camera, procedure, actor, clip/frame, X/Y/Z, facing, room flags and input release. Add headless coverage by movement family and rerun the five story routes for progression. Include Ada and Sherry following and handoff cases, Kendo and Marvin as scenario NPCs, a scripted enemy, and an animated scene object. Use rendered captures for pose and choreography acceptance.

A first static pass over 372 installed room-logic records found 145 uses of mode 9, 123 of mode 5, 116 of mode 4 and 89 of mode 0x15. These are instruction sites across the imported room variants. The audit has not resolved the selected actor at every site or measured which instructions execute. Mode 0x15 appears in both Kendo variants and in scripts for the crow corridor, Marvin and the Tyrant lab. Prioritize modes 9, 5 and 4 for the next movement pass.

The first shared correction handles mode 0x15 from the RE2 scenario dispatcher. The first operand is a heading and the second is its turn increment. Kendo procedure 30 selects Kendo in enemy slot 0 and asks him to turn toward 440 in steps of 64. The old NPC fallback placed him at room coordinates (440, 64). The new handler applies the original 128-unit angular arrival window for player and scenario NPC turns. Player destinations read the current script operands, and their heading goes back to the session. Native clip displacement and the turning pose are still pending. Leon's walk in procedure 9 uses modes 4 and 9 and still needs the native walk routines and animation sampler. The retail dispatcher initializes turn rates from actor-specific tables, including the player and NPC rows in `SPL_TBL.H`. Use those tables, the `SPL_SCE.C` routines and the shared steering helpers in `EM_SUB.C` when porting those modes.

## Kendo window attack presentation audit

The imported 1-01 variants both run procedure 10, which stages four EM1F zombies, launches the glass procedure and starts Kendo's procedure 30. Procedure 30 selects EM48 in enemy slot 0, turns it, issues direct moves 11 and 12, and starts the variant-specific procedure 31. The window regression measures the zombies' entrance, feeding and release. It does not measure Kendo's pose, texture residency or effects drawn.

- **Death pose.** The VM schedules Kendo's moves in mode 0 with flag 0x08. The stage asks for one generic stock animation per model kind and one room binding per actor slot. The stock bank wins for a non-room clip when the move index fits. The renderer otherwise only accepts a compatible room bank and silently draws the static pose if there is no usable move. A valid move index alone cannot establish that the correct source bank was selected. The catalog contains EM48 stock and CDEMD variants. Trace the resolved asset, move, frame count, pose and root position for moves 11 and 12 before changing their mapping. Add an end-to-end test that observes the selected visible clip through the feeding shot.
- **Window zombie textures.** The field renderer uploads an atlas for one model kind, then renders every other kind using a flat color sampled per face. EM48 and EM1F have separate imported model records. Selection depends on the eligible placement order rather than camera visibility. When Kendo owns the atlas, all four zombies are affected. Allocate independently resident pages and palettes per visible kind and verify texture packets for both kinds in the same shot. Check restoration when switching between field and battle VRAM.
- **Blood and gunfire.** Both imported rooms list effects 09, 08, 11, 12, 13, 14, 0A and 18 in their local bank. Procedure 13 uses local effects 11–13 for glass. The attack also requests low effect IDs 00–03 and different attachment targets. The VM looks up every effect in the local bank and rejects attachment kinds above four. The stage repeats the same room-only lookup and resolves attachments at actor origins, without part matrices. The Leon variant also uses opcodes 0x7A and 0x7B in an attack helper. Port the common effect bank and trace each cue's bank, attachment and opcode alongside the existing room sound cues. Verify the exact flash, blood and smoke assets against retail frames before assigning labels to effect IDs.

The turn fix changes procedure 30's heading and arrival. The single-model atlas and room-only effect lookup are independent of that change. Compare logs for procedure 30's arrival and the first frame of move 11 before attributing a death-pose timing defect to the turn fix.

### Implementation status

- The field renderer now keeps multiple model kinds resident and reserves each texture and palette rectangle during battle allocation. Wider imported atlases are retained. EM48's 384-pixel source texture needs three 128-pixel columns.
- The room pose renderer uses the selected room bank and its authored root offset. The installed Leon and Claire variants contain 15- and 34-frame moves 11 and 12 in their EM48 room bindings. The session regression starts procedure 30 and checks that both commands select the room bank. Frame comparison from the natural trigger remains open.
- The retail Leon disc has eight common effects in `COMMON/DATA/CORE00.ESP`, including IDs 00–03. The file has a reverse footer of block offsets. It carries animation data and native CLUT/TPage references. The companion `CORE01.TIM` is a 65536-byte raw image. Retail uploads its palette separately. The common bank needs the correct palette source, slot/part transforms and rendering before Kendo's attack sprite cues can be verified.

## Scripted enemy and boss audit

Enemy work slots already accept the shared destination step and compatible direct clips. Several entrances also depend on the enemy's own routine to animate, update a scratch field, change the camera or acknowledge defeat. Those transitions currently use encounter-specific stand-ins. The boss readiness check reads their results before handing the same room slot to battle.

Use the room procedure, actor slot, type bits, command and acknowledgment, clip frame, position, camera and encounter eligibility in each trace. For bosses, also verify that the battle result reaches the same actor and wakes the waiting script exactly once. Keep montage and cameo variants separate from combat-eligible placements.
